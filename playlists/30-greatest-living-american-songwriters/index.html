Liner Notes

why these songs

A genre-spanning, opinionated mixtape that uses thirty songs to make a single argument about who American songwriting's greatest living practitioners are — and why their craft matters.

A canon-style argument for the thirty greatest living American songwriters, the playlist deliberately moves across hip-hop, soul, folk, country, indie rock, punk, and Motown to insist that great songwriting is genre-agnostic. It pairs solo auteurs (Dylan, Springsteen, Parton, Stevie Wonder, Lana Del Rey, Adrianne Lenker, Lucinda Williams) with writers behind groups (Stipe, Westerberg, Black Francis, Smokey Robinson, Holland-Dozier-Holland for The Supremes, Byrne in Talking Heads), so the lens is always on the writing rather than the persona. The result is a punchy, opinionated tour through American vernacular song — political, romantic, regional, surreal — that reads as a single sustained essay on craft.

This playlist functions as an audio canon of the thirty greatest living American songwriters — a deliberately argumentative list that braids every major thread of American popular song into one coherent statement about craft. It opens with Kendrick Lamar's spiritual self-interrogation 'i' and moves through the junkyard surrealism of Tom Waits, the elliptical jangle of R.E.M., the Smokey Robinson aphorism of The Miracles, and the boyish folk sincerity of Jonathan Richman, before sliding into the political fury of Public Enemy and the literate electric blues of Bob Dylan's 'Queen Jane Approximately.' The middle stretch — Talking Heads, Dolly Parton, Wilco, Dion, Lana Del Rey, Neko Case, OutKast, Randy Newman, Bonnie Prince Billy — argues that craft is genre-agnostic: a Smokey couplet, a Parton hook, an André 3000 verse, and a Will Oldham hymn are all doing the same fundamental job. Patti Smith's 'Kimberly' and Big Thief's 'Mary' bookend forty years of American poet-rock; Gillian Welch and The Supremes (via Holland-Dozier-Holland) sit beside each other to remind you that the songwriter's chair has always been occupied by writers behind the singer as often as by the singer themselves. The closing run — Cass McCombs, JAY-Z, Springsteen, Lucinda Williams, Stevie Wonder, The Replacements, Pixies, Tyler The Creator, Paul Simon, Walter Martin — keeps blowing the lens wider, so that by the final track the playlist's actual subject reveals itself: not a personality contest, but a loose theory of American songcraft itself, told in thirty discrete arguments.

iii.

Songwriters

a portrait gallery

Kendrick Lamar

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Compton MC widely considered one of the most ambitious lyricists in hip-hop, writing dense narratives that braid Black American history with personal autobiography. As primary writer (and frequent co-producer) on To Pimp A Butterfly, Mr. Morale, and DAMN., his credits span jazz-rooted suites, gospel, G-funk, and confessional spoken-word. He won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 2018, the first non-classical/jazz artist to do so.

Influences
Tupac Shakur, Eazy-E, Nas, OutKast, Dr. Dre, Marvin Gaye, Parliament-Funkadelic, James Baldwin, Toni Morrison
Similar artists
J. Cole, Vince Staples, Earl Sweatshirt, Schoolboy Q, Isaiah Rashad, Joey Bada$$
Subgenre / Scene
West Coast hip-hop / conscious rap / TDE collective

Tom Waits

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Singular American songwriter whose catalog moves from boozy piano balladry (Closing Time, Small Change) to junkyard-orchestra avant-blues (Rain Dogs, Bone Machine). Co-writes most material with his wife Kathleen Brennan, and his songs have been recorded by Springsteen, Rod Stewart, Johnny Cash, and Scarlett Johansson. Equally celebrated as a character actor, his lyrics read like Beat-era short fiction set to busted carnival music.

Influences
Kurt Weill, Howlin' Wolf, Captain Beefheart, Charles Bukowski, Jack Kerouac, Harry Partch, Louis Armstrong, Ray Charles
Similar artists
Nick Cave, Captain Beefheart, Leonard Cohen, Randy Newman, Mark Lanegan, Daniel Lanois
Subgenre / Scene
Experimental Americana / junkyard blues / theatrical art-rock

R.E.M.

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Athens, GA quartet whose songs were credited collectively (Berry/Buck/Mills/Stipe) and effectively launched American college rock. Michael Stipe's elliptical, image-driven lyrics paired with Peter Buck's jangling Rickenbacker shaped a new template for indie songwriting in the 1980s and '90s. By the time of Automatic For The People, they were writing some of the most lyrically vulnerable mainstream rock songs of the era.

Influences
The Byrds, Big Star, Television, Patti Smith, Gang of Four, The Velvet Underground, Wire
Similar artists
The Replacements, Pavement, Wilco, The Smiths, Yo La Tengo, Guided by Voices
Subgenre / Scene
American college rock / jangle pop / alternative rock

The Miracles

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Detroit vocal group fronted and primarily written for by Smokey Robinson, whose songwriting gave Motown one of its most poetic voices. Robinson wrote or co-wrote most of the group's hits and produced songs for The Temptations, Mary Wells, and Marvin Gaye, earning Bob Dylan's famous (and probably joking) endorsement as 'America's greatest living poet.' 'You Never Miss A Good Thing' is a Robinson composition built on his trademark twist-of-phrase aphorisms.

Influences
Sam Cooke, Nolan Strong & The Diablos, Clyde McPhatter, Jackie Wilson, Rodgers & Hammerstein
Similar artists
The Temptations, The Four Tops, Marvin Gaye, Mary Wells, The Impressions
Subgenre / Scene
Motown / classic soul / R&B vocal groups

Jonathan Richman

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Boston-bred songwriter who fronted the proto-punk Modern Lovers before pivoting to a deliberately childlike, acoustic-driven style that's been hugely influential on indie rock's sincerity wing. His lyrics champion ice cream, old paintings, lonely diners, and faithful love, written in plain language but with an almost folkloric specificity. He's been covered by everyone from David Bowie to Joan Jett, and is the on-screen troubadour of 'There's Something About Mary.'

Influences
The VelvetUndeground, Lou Reed, Chuck Berry, Buddy Holly, doo-wop, Vincent van Gogh
Similar artists
Daniel Johnston, The Modern Lovers, Beat Happening, Violent Femmes, Calvin Johnson
Subgenre / Scene
Proto-punk / twee / lo-fi acoustic indie

Public Enemy

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Long Island rap group whose songs (mostly written by Chuck D with Flavor Flav, Hank Shocklee, and the Bomb Squad) made overtly political hip-hop a commercial and artistic force. Chuck D's writing weaponized news headlines, Black Power rhetoric, and polysyllabic internal rhyme over the Bomb Squad's collage-heavy production. 'Fight The Power,' written for Spike Lee's Do The Right Thing, remains one of the most-cited protest songs of the late 20th century.

Influences
Run-D.M.C., Gil Scott-Heron, The Last Poets, Malcolm X, James Brown, Bob Marley, Melle Mel
Similar artists
Boogie Down Productions, Rakim, X-Clan, Ice Cube, dead prez, Run the Jewels
Subgenre / Scene
Political hip-hop / golden age East Coast rap

Bob Dylan

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

The figure most responsible for turning popular song into a literary form, awarded the 2016 Nobel Prize in Literature for his songwriting. From folk-revival protest material to the surreal mid-60s electric trilogy ('Highway 61 Revisited' included) to late-period meditations on mortality, his catalog of self-written songs is the single most-covered in the rock era. 'Queen Jane Approximately' is a quintessential Dylan address-song: bitter, generous, and rhythmically off-kilter.

Influences
Woody Guthrie, Hank Williams, Robert Johnson, Arthur Rimbaud, Allen Ginsberg, the King James Bible
Similar artists
Leonard Cohen, Neil Young, Bruce Springsteen, Joni Mitchell, Townes Van Zandt, John Prine
Subgenre / Scene
Folk revival / electric folk-rock / Americana

Talking Heads

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

New York art-rock band whose songs were credited as Byrne/Frantz/Harrison/Weymouth (with Brian Eno producing/co-writing on Remain In Light). David Byrne's lyrics treat suburbia, office work, and the American body as anthropological subjects, sung in his trademark twitching deadpan. 'Found a Job,' from More Songs About Buildings and Food, is a satirical Talking Heads classic about a couple making their own TV show to save their marriage.

Influences
Al Green, Funkadelic, Velvet Underground, Roxy Music, Fela Kuti, minimalist composers like Steve Reich
Similar artists
Devo, B-52's, LCD Soundsystem, Tom Tom Club, Television, David Byrne solo
Subgenre / Scene
CBGB-era new wave / art-punk / post-punk

Dolly Parton

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Nashville icon whose songwriting catalog is staggering: 'Jolene,' 'I Will Always Love You,' 'Coat of Many Colors,' '9 to 5,' and roughly 3,000 more compositions. She writes character-driven country songs grounded in her East Tennessee upbringing, and is one of the very few country writers to also dominate as a publisher and businesswoman. 'Gonna Hurry (As Slow As I Can)' is a demo-era Parton original showcasing her gift for plainspoken, hooky storytelling.

Influences
Hank Williams, Kitty Wells, Porter Wagoner, the Carter Family, Appalachian folk, gospel hymns
Similar artists
Loretta Lynn, Tammy Wynette, Emmylou Harris, Reba McEntire, Tanya Tucker
Subgenre / Scene
Classic Nashville country / countrypolitan / Appalachian-rooted songwriting

Wilco

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Chicago band led by Jeff Tweedy, whose songwriting shifted from Uncle Tupelo-style alt-country into the studio-as-instrument experimentalism of Yankee Hotel Foxtrot. Tweedy writes most of the material (often with the late Jay Bennett on YHF), drawing on Beatles-era pop craft, Krautrock textures, and confessional balladry. 'Jesus, Etc.' is one of his most-covered songs and a key example of his fragmented, image-rich lyric style.

Influences
The Beatles, Big Star, Neil Young, The Band, Krautrock (Can, Neu!), Woody Guthrie
Similar artists
Uncle Tupelo, Son Volt, The Jayhawks, Whiskeytown, My Morning Jacket, The National
Subgenre / Scene
Alt-country / Americana / experimental indie rock

Dion

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Bronx-born rock and roll pioneer (Dion DiMucci) who fronted Dion and the Belmonts before a long solo career as a writer/singer. He co-wrote much of his 1960s material and has continued releasing self-written blues records into his 80s, with collaborators ranging from Paul Simon to Bruce Springsteen. 'Love Came To Me' is a Dion original from 1962 that helped bridge doo-wop's vocal stylings into singer-songwriter territory.

Influences
Hank Williams, Jimmy Reed, Frankie Lymon, doo-wop street-corner harmony, Robert Johnson
Similar artists
The Belmonts, Frankie Valli, Del Shannon, Roy Orbison, Lou Christie
Subgenre / Scene
Doo-wop / early rock and roll / blues-based singer-songwriter

Lana Del Rey

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

American songwriter (Elizabeth Grant) who built a self-mythologizing catalog around old-Hollywood imagery, Americana, and slowcore-tempo balladry. She co-writes nearly all her material — most notably with Jack Antonoff and Rick Nowels — weaving allusions to Whitman, Ginsberg, and Nabokov into songs about California, doomed romance, and celebrity. 'A&W' is a sprawling, two-part Antonoff co-write often cited as her most ambitious song to date.

Influences
Leonard Cohen, Bruce Springsteen, Nina Simone, Allen Ginsberg, Sylvia Plath, Nancy Sinatra, hip-hop production
Similar artists
Father John Misty, Weyes Blood, Mitski, Ethel Cain, Angel Olsen
Subgenre / Scene
Indie pop / baroque pop / Americana-tinged alt-pop

Neko Case

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Singer-songwriter (and member of The New Pornographers) whose solo writing pulls country noir into surreal, image-dense territory. She writes nearly all her own material, with lyrics that read like compressed short fiction — tornadoes, foxes, murderers, and lost women recurring as motifs. 'Lion's Jaws,' from Fox Confessor Brings the Flood, is a typical Case lyric: cryptic, propulsive, and emotionally direct.

Influences
Loretta Lynn, Roy Orbison, Buffy Sainte-Marie, Tom Waits, Carter Family, Iris DeMent
Similar artists
Gillian Welch, Aimee Mann, Jenny Lewis, Kelly Hogan, The New Pornographers, Calexico
Subgenre / Scene
Alt-country / country noir / indie folk

Outkast

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Atlanta duo (André 3000 and Big Boi) credited as primary writers on most of their catalog, who pushed Southern hip-hop from regional curiosity to one of the most innovative acts of their era. Their songwriting fuses funk, gospel, P-funk, soul, and electro with ambitious concept-album structures (Stankonia, Speakerboxxx/The Love Below). 'So Fresh, So Clean,' co-produced by Organized Noize, is a model of their playful, hook-loaded craft.

Influences
Parliament-Funkadelic, Prince, Sly & The Family Stone, A Tribe Called Quest, Curtis Mayfield, the Dungeon Family
Similar artists
Goodie Mob, Big Boi solo, André 3000 solo, Organized Noize, T.I., Janelle Monáe
Subgenre / Scene
Southern hip-hop / Dungeon Family / funk-rap

Randy Newman

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

American songwriter and film composer whose songs use unreliable narrators to satirize racism, empire, and small-mindedness ('Sail Away,' 'Rednecks,' 'Political Science'). He has written nearly his entire catalog solo, plus film scores for Pixar, Disney, and others, winning multiple Oscars. 'Wandering Boy,' from Dark Matter, is a late-career Newman ballad sung in his own voice rather than in character.

Influences
Stephen Foster, George Gershwin, Fats Domino, Allen Toussaint, Ray Charles, Kurt Weill, his uncles Alfred and Lionel Newman (film composers)
Similar artists
Harry Nilsson, Tom Waits, Rufus Wainwright, Loudon Wainwright III, Burt Bacharach
Subgenre / Scene
Tin Pan Alley revivalism / piano-based singer-songwriter / film composition

Bonnie Prince Billy

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Will Oldham's primary recording alias, under which he writes spare, hymn-like songs that traverse mortality, faith, sex, and Appalachian dread. He writes essentially all his own material and has had songs covered by Johnny Cash ('I See A Darkness') and Mark Kozelek, among many others. 'What Are You?' is a co-write with Matt Sweeney from Superwolf, the duo's first album.

Influences
Carter Family, Roscoe Holcomb, Leonard Cohen, Townes Van Zandt, John Fahey, Appalachian shape-note hymnody
Similar artists
Smog/Bill Callahan, Will Oldham (Palace), Songs: Ohia / Jason Molina, Iron & Wine, David Berman/Silver Jews
Subgenre / Scene
Indie folk / freak folk / Drag City alt-country

Patti Smith

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Punk-poet whose 1975 debut Horses fused beat-poetry recitation with garage rock and effectively created the singer-as-poet template for punk. She writes most of her material (often with longtime guitarist Lenny Kaye and the late Fred 'Sonic' Smith) and has won the National Book Award for her memoir Just Kids. 'Kimberly,' written for her younger sister, is a key Horses track demonstrating her lyric-as-incantation approach.

Influences
Arthur Rimbaud, William Blake, Allen Ginsberg, Bob Dylan, The Rolling Stones, John Coltrane, Jim Morrison
Similar artists
Television, Richard Hell, Lou Reed, PJ Harvey, Sonic Youth, The Pretenders
Subgenre / Scene
CBGB-era punk / poetry-rock / proto-punk

Big Thief

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Brooklyn-based indie folk band whose songs are written almost entirely by Adrianne Lenker, one of the most acclaimed lyricists of her generation. Lenker's writing — drawn from a turbulent rural upbringing — pairs finger-picked folk arrangements with imagistic, nervy lyrics about family, queerness, and time. 'Mary,' from Capacity, is an emotionally direct ballad praised as among her most powerful early songs.

Influences
Elliott Smith, Joni Mitchell, Karen Dalton, Nick Drake, Townes Van Zandt, Bob Dylan
Similar artists
Adrianne Lenker solo, Buck Meek, Florist, Indigo De Souza, Waxahatchee, Phoebe Bridgers
Subgenre / Scene
Indie folk / contemporary singer-songwriter / 4AD/Saddle Creek-adjacent indie

Gillian Welch

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

American songwriter who, with longtime partner David Rawlings, has written all of her catalog as a duo even though it's released under her name. Their writing recreates the rhythms and vocabulary of pre-war country, gospel, and Appalachian ballad while grappling with thoroughly modern subjects (the music industry, addiction, regret). 'Dear Someone,' from Time (The Revelator), is a typical Welch/Rawlings composition — sparse, harmonized, and modal.

Influences
The Carter Family, The Stanley Brothers, Doc Watson, Hank Williams, Neil Young, Bob Dylan
Similar artists
Dave Rawlings Machine, Iris DeMent, Alison Krauss, Welch's collaborators on O Brother Where Art Thou, Bonnie Prince Billy
Subgenre / Scene
Old-time / Americana / neo-traditional country

The Supremes

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Detroit vocal trio (Diana Ross, Florence Ballard, Mary Wilson) who became Motown's most commercially successful act, with most of their hits written and produced by Holland-Dozier-Holland. The group itself didn't write the songs, but their interpretation of HDH's catalog defined the Motown sound and made them the first Black act to dominate American pop charts. 'Baby Love' is one of HDH's most efficient pop compositions, sung by Ross.

Influences
The Andrews Sisters, doo-wop groups, gospel quartet singing, Sam Cooke, the Motown house band (the Funk Brothers)
Similar artists
The Marvelettes, Martha & The Vandellas, The Ronettes, The Shirelles, The Crystals
Subgenre / Scene
Motown / 60s girl group / classic pop-soul

Cass McCombs

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Concord, CA-born songwriter known for understated, literary songs that range from country-rock and dub to baroque ballads. He writes everything himself and has long been a critics' favorite for his oblique, novelistic lyrics. 'That's That,' from Dropping the Writ, is one of his most upbeat songs while staying lyrically slippery in his typical fashion.

Influences
Bob Dylan, Townes Van Zandt, The Grateful Dead, Lee 'Scratch' Perry, Arthur Russell, Donovan
Similar artists
Bill Callahan, Kurt Vile, The War on Drugs, Bonnie Prince Billy, Damien Jurado
Subgenre / Scene
Indie folk / Americana / lo-fi singer-songwriter

JAŸ-Z

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Brooklyn rap mogul (Shawn Carter) widely regarded as one of the greatest lyricists in the genre, famous for composing entire albums in his head without writing lyrics down. His writing covers his crack-era youth, his rise as a label founder (Roc-A-Fella, Roc Nation), and married life with Beyoncé. 'No Church In The Wild,' from Watch The Throne with Kanye West, is a Frank Ocean–assisted track exploring power and skepticism, with songwriting credited to him, West, Ocean, and producers The 88-Keys / 88-Keys-camp.

Influences
The Notorious B.I.G., Rakim, Jaz-O, Kool G Rap, LL Cool J, Big Daddy Kane
Similar artists
Nas, Notorious B.I.G., Kanye West, Beanie Sigel, Memphis Bleek, Jay Electronica
Subgenre / Scene
East Coast hip-hop / mafioso rap / luxury rap

Bruce Springsteen

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

New Jersey songwriter whose body of work — from working-class character studies (Darkness on the Edge of Town) to barroom epics (Born to Run) to stripped folk records (Nebraska) — is one of the defining American catalogs of the rock era. He writes virtually all his songs solo, with the E Street Band as primary interpreters. '4th of July, Asbury Park (Sandy)' is an early showcase of his Phil-Spector-meets-Dylan boardwalk-romantic mode.

Influences
Bob Dylan, Roy Orbison, Phil Spector productions, Hank Williams, Woody Guthrie, Van Morrison, Elvis Presley
Similar artists
Tom Petty, John Mellencamp, The Gaslight Anthem, Bob Seger, Steve Earle, The Killers
Subgenre / Scene
Heartland rock / Americana / E Street rock & roll

Lucinda Williams

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Louisiana-born songwriter celebrated for fusing Southern country, blues, and rock into deeply specific, place-rich lyrics. She writes nearly everything herself and is famously unhurried about it (Car Wheels on a Gravel Road took six years). 'Jackson' is a quiet centerpiece of that record — a slow, geographic lament that names towns the way her best songs always do.

Influences
Hank Williams, Robert Johnson, Bob Dylan, Loretta Lynn, Memphis Minnie, Flannery O'Connor
Similar artists
Emmylou Harris, Steve Earle, Rosanne Cash, Patty Griffin, Mary Gauthier, Buddy Miller
Subgenre / Scene
Americana / alt-country / Southern singer-songwriter

Stevie Wonder

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

One of the most prolific and harmonically sophisticated songwriters in popular music, who took full creative control of his catalog at age 21 and produced the run of Talking Book / Innervisions / Fulfillingness' / Songs in the Key of Life. He writes (and typically plays most instruments on) the bulk of his own material. 'I Wish' is a Songs In The Key Of Life autobiographical funk composition built on his own keyboard, drum, and bass tracks.

Influences
Ray Charles, Sam Cooke, Marvin Gaye, gospel music, jazz harmony, The Beatles
Similar artists
Marvin Gaye, Donny Hathaway, Sly Stone, Prince, D'Angelo, The Isley Brothers
Subgenre / Scene
Motown / classic soul / funk / R&B auteur

The Replacements

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Minneapolis band whose songwriter Paul Westerberg gave 1980s American underground rock its most ragged, bighearted lyric voice. He wrote nearly every song, mixing punk speed, Stones-y rock, and country balladry — and his vulnerable, self-deprecating writing on Tim and Let It Be heavily shaped 90s alt-rock. 'Androgynous,' from Let It Be, is a quiet, empathetic Westerberg piano ballad about gender presentation that was decades ahead of its lyrical moment.

Influences
Big Star, Alex Chilton, The Faces, Hank Williams, Hüsker Dü, The Rolling Stones, Slade
Similar artists
Hüsker Dü, Soul Asylum, Big Star, Goo Goo Dolls (early), Paul Westerberg solo, The Hold Steady
Subgenre / Scene
American underground rock / Twin/Tone Records / proto-alt-rock

Pixies

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Boston band whose songs (chiefly written by Black Francis / Charles Thompson, with Kim Deal contributing key tracks) used loud-quiet-loud dynamics, Spanish-language imagery, and surrealist Bible-and-sci-fi lyrics to reset the template for alternative rock. Surfer Rosa, produced by Steve Albini, became the playbook for Nirvana and dozens of 90s bands. 'Cactus' is a Black Francis composition later covered by David Bowie on Heathen.

Influences
Hüsker Dü, Iggy Pop, Buddy Holly, surf rock, Captain Beefheart, biblical imagery
Similar artists
Frank Black, The Breeders, Sonic Youth, Nirvana, Throwing Muses, Weezer
Subgenre / Scene
College rock / alternative rock / loud-quiet-loud indie

Tyler, The Creator

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

Los Angeles producer/rapper (Tyler Okonma) who founded the Odd Future collective and writes/produces nearly all his material — increasingly venturing into Pharrell-influenced soul/jazz arrangements (Flower Boy, IGOR, Call Me If You Get Lost). His lyrics move between bratty provocation, queer longing, and luxurious self-mythology. 'ARE WE STILL FRIENDS?' is the IGOR closer, a Stevie-Wonder-indebted ballad demonstrating his maturation as a writer-arranger.

Influences
Pharrell Williams / The Neptunes, Stevie Wonder, Roy Ayers, MF DOOM, Eminem, Kanye West
Similar artists

Paul Simon

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

American songwriter who first achieved fame with Simon & Garfunkel and went on to a solo catalog (Graceland, Rhythm of the Saints, Hearts and Bones) celebrated for marrying literate, often allusive lyrics with global musical idioms. He writes virtually everything himself but is known for collaborating closely with musicians from South Africa, Brazil, and West Africa as co-arrangers. 'The Obvious Child,' from The Rhythm of The Saints, is built around the Brazilian percussion ensemble Grupo Cultural Olodum.

Influences
The Everly Brothers, doo-wop, Bob Dylan, Brazilian samba, South African mbaqanga, Ladysmith Black Mambazo

Walter Martin

Last.fm ↗ Spotify ↗

American songwriter best known as an original member of The Walkmen, who has built a quietly acclaimed solo career writing low-key, conversational songs about art, family, and travel. He writes everything himself and frequently collaborates with friends like Karen O, Matt Berninger, and members of The Walkmen on guest vocals. 'The Soldier,' from The World at Night, is a typical Martin track — gentle, nostalgic, and lightly funny.

Influences
Jonathan Richman, Lou Reed, The Walkmen, Harry Nilsson, classic American folk and children's records
Similar artists
The Walkmen, Hamilton Leithauser, Matt Berninger / The National, Kevin Morby, Bill Callahan
Subgenre / Scene
Indie folk / chamber indie / The Walkmen extended family

Liner Notes

Doors opening one
after another

"Spring Is Pushing" is an omnivorous, deeply personal mixtape that moves like a conversation between decades, continents, and moods. It opens with the swaying Creole waltz of Loulou Boislaville's Martinican carnival music and closes with Miriam Makeba's jubilant "Pata Pata," bookending the playlist with diasporic joy.


In between, the collection ricochets from Kim Deal's sun-bleached indie rock coast to Little Simz's hard-hitting London grime, from the proto-punk swagger of The Troggs and Jonathan Fire*Eater to the slow-burning heartbreak of Lucy Dacus and Sharon Van Etten. Monaleo's Houston trap sits comfortably alongside Bob Dylan's 1974 folk-rock weariness and General Public's ska-kissed new wave.


Pedro The Lion's slowcore introspection gives way to Jonathan Richman's disarming live monologue, while Blondshell and Bully rage through '90s-flavored grunge with confessional urgency. The playlist's emotional center lives in its singer-songwriters—John Prine's tender Americana, Jenny Lewis's self-examining indie pop, Cass McCombs's cryptic baroque folk, and Walter Martin's wry storytelling.


The sequencing feels like spring itself—unpredictable, restless, warming in waves—with each track pushing open a different door.

Louis Boislaville
Martinican Folklore

Louis-Lucien "Loulou" Boislaville (1919–2001) was a Martinican singer, actor, and artistic director who devoted his life to promoting Creole tradition and folklore. He founded the Groupe Folklorique Martiniquais in 1946 and later directed the Grand Ballet of Martinique, becoming a key figure in preserving the music and dance legacy of the French West Indies.
